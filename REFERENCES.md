# Further reading and reliable starting points

The Mountain Atlas is an educational introduction rather than a substitute for regional scientific data, emergency services or professional advice. These public educational and assessment sources are useful starting points:

- U.S. Geological Survey — Geology Education: https://www.usgs.gov/educational-resources/geology-education
- U.S. Geological Survey — Natural Hazards Education: https://www.usgs.gov/educational-resources/natural-hazards-education
- U.S. Geological Survey — Volcano Hazards Program education resources: https://www.usgs.gov/programs/VHP/education-resources
- U.S. National Park Service — Glacier National Park geology: https://home.nps.gov/glac/learn/education/geology.htm
- Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change — Mountains, AR6 WGII: https://www.ipcc.ch/report/ar6/wg2/chapter/ccp5/
- FAO — Mountain Partnership: https://www.fao.org/mountain-partnership/en
- FAO — Mountain farming: https://www.fao.org/family-farming/themes/mountain-farming/en/
- FAO — Water and mountains: https://www.fao.org/forestry-fao/watershedmanagementandmountains/90329/en/
- UNESCO World Heritage Centre: https://whc.unesco.org/
- Global Mountain Biodiversity Assessment: https://www.gmba.unibe.ch/

When working in the field, prioritise current local weather, avalanche, volcano, protected-area and emergency information. Not every species or process described here occurs in every range.
