# The Alaska Range

Explore a high-latitude mountain system shaped by tectonics, glaciers, extreme weather and large connected wilderness areas.

> **Atlas note:** This article is a learning introduction. Conditions differ across mountain ranges; use regional sources for local decisions.

## A high-latitude mountain wall

The Alaska Range arcs across southern Alaska and includes Denali, North America’s highest summit above sea level. Its landscapes are shaped by active tectonics, intense glaciation, large elevation gradients and weather systems arriving from the Pacific and interior. The range transitions from forest and shrubland to tundra, bare rock, snow and ice.

## Glaciers and mountain weather

Many glaciers flow from high accumulation zones into valleys, carrying sediment and reshaping terrain. Weather can be highly variable, with wind, cloud, cold and storms making high elevations dangerous even during a short summer season. The scale of the range, limited access and rapid weather changes make route planning and reliable communication especially important.

## Wildlife and seasonal movement

Caribou, Dall sheep, bears, wolves, migratory birds and smaller mammals use different habitat bands and seasonal resources. Large protected landscapes can provide room for movement, but roads, development and climate change can still influence access to food, migration routes and denning or nesting habitat.

## Observation and stewardship

The Alaska Range is a useful case study for glacial monitoring, permafrost, high-latitude ecology and wilderness management. Visitors should respect closures, wildlife distance, local rules and the limits of rescue access. Remote landscapes are not risk-free merely because they appear empty or undisturbed.
