# The Drakensberg

Discover basalt escarpments, grassland plateaus, seasonal storms and distinctive mountain biodiversity in southern Africa.

> **Atlas note:** This article is a learning introduction. Conditions differ across mountain ranges; use regional sources for local decisions.

## Escarpment and plateau

The Drakensberg forms part of the Great Escarpment of southern Africa, where high basalt terrain and deeply incised valleys create dramatic cliffs and plateaus. Its landforms reflect volcanic rock layers, uplift and long-term erosion. Elevation alters temperature and precipitation, producing conditions that contrast with surrounding lowlands.

## Grasslands and water

Montane grassland, rocky outcrops, wetlands and stream headwaters support varied plant and animal communities. Grassland fire and grazing patterns help shape vegetation, but timing and intensity matter; altered fire regimes, invasive plants and erosion can change habitat structure. Water from the highlands supports ecological and human needs downstream.

## Cultural heritage

The region holds deep cultural significance and archaeological heritage, including rock-art sites associated with San histories. Sites must be treated respectfully and protected from vandalism, copying, removal and unapproved disclosure of sensitive details. Landscape interpretation should draw on heritage specialists and relevant communities.

## Pressures and management

Tourism, livestock, agriculture, water infrastructure and climate variability create management challenges. Protected areas, responsible visitation, fire planning, erosion control and community collaboration can support the persistence of both biodiversity and cultural heritage.
