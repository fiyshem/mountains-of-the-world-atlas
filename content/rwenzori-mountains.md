# The Rwenzori Mountains

Study the “Mountains of the Moon”: equatorial glaciers, bogs, giant groundsel, cloud forest and unusual high-altitude habitats.

> **Atlas note:** This article is a learning introduction. Conditions differ across mountain ranges; use regional sources for local decisions.

## High mountains near the equator

The Rwenzori range lies along the Uganda–Democratic Republic of the Congo border region. Its high peaks rise above humid forest and montane vegetation into bogs, giant herbs, rock and formerly more extensive ice. The presence of glaciers at equatorial latitude reflects the height of the summits and specialised local climate, rather than the latitude alone.

## Distinctive vegetation belts

Montane forest gives way to bamboo and other belts in some locations, followed by heather, bog vegetation and giant rosette plants at high elevations. Moisture, cloud immersion, soils and disturbance contribute to this unusual arrangement. Plants adapted to cool high-altitude conditions can be vulnerable when temperature, rainfall or habitat structure changes.

## Ice and changing climate

The range’s small tropical glaciers have been shrinking, making it a striking example of cryosphere change in a tropical setting. Glacier loss interacts with rainfall and catchment processes, so it should not be treated as the sole driver of water availability. Long-term photography, remote sensing and field measurements help document change.

## Access and stewardship

The terrain is wet, rugged and physically demanding, and safe travel depends on local guidance, permits, weather and route conditions. Conservation must address forest, bog and alpine habitats together with nearby communities and tourism systems. This is a landscape where the transition from rainforest to high alpine environments occurs over a relatively compact distance.
