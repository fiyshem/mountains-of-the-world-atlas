The Ural Mountains extend from the Arctic region toward northern Kazakhstan. They are an old mountain system that has undergone extensive erosion, leaving many rounded ridges and forested slopes.

The Urals are often treated as part of the conventional geographic boundary between Europe and Asia. Their ecosystems include tundra, taiga, mixed forest, wetlands, and mountain grasslands.

## Mammals and birds

- Eurasian brown bear (Ursus arctos): a large omnivore of forest and mountain habitats.
- Eurasian lynx (Lynx lynx): a predator associated with forested landscapes.
- Reindeer (Rangifer tarandus): occurs in suitable northern habitats.
- Eurasian elk or moose (Alces alces): a large browser of forests and wetlands.
- Western capercaillie (Tetrao urogallus): a large grouse associated with suitable forest habitats.
- Black grouse (Lyrurus tetrix): a bird of woodland edges, clearings, and open landscapes.
- Ural owl (Strix uralensis): a forest owl with a broad Eurasian distribution.

## Trees and plants

- Siberian spruce (Picea obovata): a major conifer of northern forests.
- Siberian fir (Abies sibirica): an evergreen tree of cool continental forests.
- Silver birch (Betula pendula): a deciduous tree of open woodland and disturbed habitats.
- Scots pine (Pinus sylvestris): a widely distributed Eurasian conifer.
- Bilberry (Vaccinium myrtillus): a low-growing shrub of many cool forest and heathland habitats.

The Urals demonstrate how geological history, continental climate, and forest distribution combine to produce broad ecological transitions.

## A transition across habitats and climates

The Urals extend through several climatic settings, from northern landscapes with tundra and sparse vegetation to extensive forest belts farther south. This north–south transition makes the range useful for studying how temperature, growing season, soils and moisture affect the distribution of plant and animal communities. The ridges may be lower and more rounded than in young high mountain systems, but their geography still influences local drainage and habitat patterns.

The Urals are commonly used as a conventional geographic boundary between Europe and Asia, although continental boundaries are partly matters of geographic convention. For ecology, it is often more useful to study the actual habitat and movement corridor than to assume that a continental label creates a sharp biological division. Many species have distributions extending across large parts of northern Eurasia.

## Forests, resources and long-term change

Taiga, mixed woodland, wetlands and open mountain habitats support large mammals and forest birds. Mining, transport infrastructure, forestry and settlement can influence land cover, water quality and habitat connection in some parts of the range. The scale and intensity of these effects vary by location, so regional evidence is essential.

A comparative field exercise could follow a transect from a wooded lower slope toward an open ridge. Record tree composition, soil moisture, wind exposure and visible evidence of disturbance. Compare the observations with long-term climate or vegetation information and ask whether the pattern is controlled primarily by elevation, latitude, geology or human activity. In older ranges, a gentler profile can still conceal a complex ecological story.
